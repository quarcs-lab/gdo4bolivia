## The 343-unit map and DS4Bolivia's polygon data

The municipal map changed between GeoBolivia's 2015 map (339 municipalities, the basis of DS4Bolivia) and SDSN Bolivia's 2025 map (343 units). Four units were created: TIOC‑Raqaypampa (from Mizque), San Pedro de Macha (from Colquechaca), TIOC‑Jatun Ayllu Yura (from Tomave and Porco) and TIOC‑Territorio Indígena Multiétnico (from San Ignacio and Santa Ana, as a new Beni province). The other 333 municipalities match their 2015 polygons (area overlap above 0.99). The data DS4Bolivia computed on its polygons (`pop`, `ntl`, `emb`, `embpw`) therefore apply only to those 333; the six municipalities that lost territory and the four new units have no rows.

## DS4Bolivia's swapped homonyms

Three pairs of municipalities share a name across departments: San Javier (Beni, Santa Cruz), El Puente (Tarija, Santa Cruz) and Santa Rosa (Beni, Pando). In DS4Bolivia, the attribute rows of each pair (name, department, INE code, and the SDG Atlas 2020 variables joined by code) are attached to the other polygon of the pair, while the data computed from the polygons (population, night-time lights, satellite embeddings) belong to the polygon. The evidence:

- the polygon of `asdf_id` 57 lies in Beni, but its row says San Javier, Santa Cruz (`mun_id` 71102), with an Atlas population of 16,027, the size of San Javier in Santa Cruz (its WorldPop population is 8,824)
- after swapping the rows back, every INE department and province code matches exactly one GADM department and province; before, six municipalities broke that nesting

`lookups/municipalities339_gadm.csv` records both keys (`swapped = True` for the six), and the polygon-based builders join on `asdf_id`. The Atlas 2026 is linked by INE code, so the swap does not affect it.
